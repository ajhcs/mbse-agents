# HG-10-CH1 — joint readiness note

**Recommendation: reject separate departmental briefings as a replacement for the joint exception-handling rehearsal. Hold opening of the affected baggage-dependent service until an integrated trial and its unresolved issues have been reviewed.** A fixed public date is a planning constraint, not evidence of readiness. A demonstrably separable reduced service may be proposed for that date, but its staffing, route, capacity, passenger arrangements and fallback must be bounded and exercised first. None of those conditions is established by this packet.

This is a synthetic readiness decision, not a retrospective approval or a claim about what Heathrow should have done on a particular date. HG-10-F1–F2 are attributed historical findings; the interfaces and claims are teaching reconstructions. No trial has been run here, and no public passengers, systems, or operations have been affected. Proposed decision roles below are role names, not appointments already made.

The inspected [Transport Committee report, chapter 2, paragraphs 8–14](https://publications.parliament.uk/pa/cm200708/cmselect/cmtran/543/54304.htm) supports the narrow training/familiarisation finding. Committee conclusions are distinguishable from witness accounts about training and bag diversity. The inspected [chapter 3, paragraphs 15–19](https://publications.parliament.uk/pa/cm200708/cmselect/cmtran/543/54305.htm) records communication concerns and joint-working arrangements, but does not establish with certainty that earlier cooperation would have prevented all problems. These sources motivate scenario coverage; they supply no capacity target for the fictional opening.

A briefing can support a claim that identified staff received a specified procedure revision if delivery and attendance are recorded. A separately observed knowledge check could support a narrower claim that those staff can identify their own action, contact and escalation rule. A list of departmental questions can expose open concerns. Neither briefing proposal nor attendance proves those outcomes, and no such records are supplied.

Only an integrated observation can show that airline and operator staff exchange the right information, acknowledge custody and authority, recognize disruption, coordinate passenger/bag decisions, and resume without losing status. Representative physical work is also needed to show staff can find and operate the actual spaces and equipment under HG-10-I1 and HG-10-I4. A tabletop rehearsal can expose authority gaps but cannot establish physical route performance, equipment handling, staffing adequacy or throughput.

## Proposed trial and decision roles

Run the three scenarios in `scenarios.md` as a joint control-room/tabletop exercise now, using controlled simulated bag/passenger identities and actual planned communication channels where available. Record every simulation and substitution. The airline duty manager and terminal duty manager must participate with representative front-line staff, baggage/handling roles and an observer from each organization. No live service action is authorized by this plan.

Once an isolated, safely available route representative of the proposed opening scope exists, repeat the scenarios as a physical end-to-end exercise using the actual relevant configuration, work positions, interfaces and test bags. Facility delivery must document which spaces, equipment, staffing conditions and bag characteristics are represented. If only a reduced route is available, the result can apply only to that route and those conditions. If no representative route is available before opening, do not replace that missing evidence with a tabletop success: delay the dependent service. Trial feasibility and facility availability are themselves unresolved.

Before either stage, the proposed joint readiness chair agrees the procedure, scope, acceptance conditions, stop rules, recorders and review time with both duty managers. Qualitative conditions include completed acknowledged handovers, consistent bag/passenger status, a named owner for every exception, correct restriction of new acceptance during disruption, and coordinated restart after reconciliation. Demand, service limits, response times, workload and capacity criteria remain to be supplied before the physical trial can substantiate capacity or timeliness. A bounded exercise is not exhaustive readiness validation.

## Options for the opening decision

| Option | Service effect and possible advantage | Evidence that discriminates it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Full scope on the public date | Keeps the planned service offer but exposes the whole affected journey to unresolved coordination and familiarisation gaps. | Representative full-scope nominal and exception trial; demand and bag mix; staffing competence/coverage; physical route readiness; integrated capacity and recovery evidence. Unsupported now. |
| Reduced initial scope | Limits flights, routes, hours or service types to an explicitly chosen demonstrable subset. May require rebooking, rerouting, longer waits, or withholding some checked-bag services. No specific reduction is selected without data. | Proof the subset is physically and operationally separable; demand and capacity for it and any receiving service; roster and fallback resources; trial of its normal/recovery journeys; passenger communications; defined expansion gate. An untested manual workaround is not a fallback. |
| Delay affected service | Preserves time for integrated familiarisation and correction, while imposing service disruption, possible rebooking and resource/scheduling costs. A delay does not itself fix the issues. | Earliest representative-area availability; corrective-work and trial duration; availability/capacity of alternatives; passenger commitments; operational and commercial consequences. Recommended default while the acceptance gaps remain. |

No weights, throughput numbers, historical airport metrics or invented financial estimates are used. The airline operations lead must supply demand, passenger commitments and alternate-service feasibility; the terminal operations lead must supply facility/capacity and route dependencies; staffing leads must supply competency and roster coverage; the joint chair must establish the decision deadline and permitted service envelope.

**Joint readiness message:** The airline and terminal operator should retain a readiness hold for the affected baggage service; separate briefings cannot close HG-10-C2 or HG-10-C3. The two duty managers own the integrated recovery procedure, facility delivery owns availability/representativeness, and the joint readiness chair owns the opening-scope decision. Reopen at the earlier of a material scope/configuration change or delivery of the representative trial record, issue dispositions, and a viable fallback/service envelope, and in all cases before the communicated go/no-go deadline. If those artifacts remain unavailable at that deadline, delay the affected service or retain only a separately evidenced subset; do not silently carry the open claims into full opening.
