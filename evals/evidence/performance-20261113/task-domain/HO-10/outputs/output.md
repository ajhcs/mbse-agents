3. **Scenario and hypothesis matrix.** Pair daylight/low-light runs, dark/light targets and contrasting backgrounds; then probe lighting-transition, headlamp/glare, wet/fogged/dirty windscreen, crossing speed/occlusion and distance boundaries. Use safe surrogate targets in a controlled facility. Choose combinations from the incident mechanism and intended envelope, rather than a nominal average success percentage. Include false-positive interventions as well as misses. Record which factors are controlled and where interactions are deliberately combined.
4. **Separate malfunction and dependency evidence.** Characterize the diagnostic's detectable fault set and detection/reaction timing using representative injected faults. Examine degraded or intermittent common converter output, camera loss/stale frames, shared compute overload and calibration errors where applicable. Include simultaneous and latent dependencies; injection coverage alone cannot prove the absence of other faults.
5. **Mitigation comparison.** Compare current and dual-camera configurations against the same challenging scenarios and independent oracle. Assess proposals such as a changed optical/perception design, appropriately different sensing, an enforceable operating restriction or revised system behavior. None is accepted in advance: diversity needs evidence, and an operating restriction needs a way to recognize and handle the boundary. 6. **Predeclared outcome and responsibility checks.** Derive the required detection range, latency and intervention/warning timing from the actual vehicle response and scenario; values remain unresolved. Measure sensor-to-physical-response timing, collision/clearance outcome, misses and false positives by condition, with sample size and uncertainty rationale. Assess whether a driver can understand the indication and respond within the available time and visibility. A warning does not transfer responsibility automatically or create time the driver lacks.
