This proposed operating concept preserves the center's priority-approval role and each supply organization's authority over its own resources. It is a draft for agreement and exercise, not an existing agreement or authorization to dispatch.

The supplied facts are: a joint coordination center, three independently managed teams, delivery of approved medical supplies after a flood, potentially six hours without communications, limited vehicle capacity and different stock records. No delegation, priority expiry rule, dispute procedure or shared inventory authority was supplied. The concept therefore distinguishes what is known from arrangements the participants still need to accept.

| Actor | Established boundary | Proposed operational arrangement requiring agreement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coordination center | Approves priorities. | Issues versioned approved demand/priority records, reconciles unmet demand and proposes allocations. Changes to priorities remain attributable to the center. Approval of a priority does not itself compel another organization's resource commitment. |
| Teams A, B and C | Independently managed; each has limited capacity and its own stock records. | Each confirms available stock, vehicles, staff, route feasibility and supply handling capability; accepts, partially accepts or declines a proposed task with reasons. Each retains control of its own dispatch and resource records. |
| Receiving site / authorized recipient | Delivery requires a destination and receipt; their identity and authority are not supplied. | Confirm a recipient and delivery acknowledgement method before dispatch; record delivered quantity and exceptions. Clinical need/approval roles must be identified rather than assigned to drivers. |
| Each organization's designated decision-maker | No shared superior or dispute arbiter is established. | Nominate contacts and alternates for resource, supply-quality and priority disputes; approve outage delegation and restoration rules within their actual authority. |

The operational boundary includes demand/priorities, offer/commitment, load/dispatch, delivery acknowledgement and reconciliation. It depends on external routes, destination accessibility, supply approval and each organization's logistics systems. It does not authorize new supplies, clinical decisions, commandeering resources, surveillance or weapons tasks.

During connected operation, the center issues an identified priority version. Teams respond with feasible commitments. A proposed shared record carries task ID, destination/recipient, approved supply identifier, quantity and unit, priority version and author, issue time/time basis, validity conditions, assigned/accepting team, stock reservation and delivery status. Local item codes map to this exchange vocabulary while preserving original codes and provenance. Different units, packaging or uncertain quantities are discrepancies to resolve, not numbers to merge silently. The accepting team checks capacity and handling constraints before committing; partial fulfillment returns an explicit unmet balance to the center.

Before an outage, participants need an agreed offline operating envelope: which already accepted tasks may continue, whether local substitutions or reprioritization are permitted, what supply/route changes require renewed approval, and what makes a priority stale. The possible six-hour outage is a resilience scenario, **not an automatic six-hour validity period**. If validity cannot be established or a changed condition invalidates a task, this draft proposes holding the affected new commitment and recording unmet need until authority is restored. Any emergency exception must come from an identified existing policy or an expressly approved delegation; none is invented here. Safe custody and actions already in progress remain subject to the responsible organization's actual procedures.

SCN-01 is one proposed degraded-operation exercise. Its extra events are synthetic injects, not facts about an actual flood response.

| Step / state | Action, observation and exchange | Authority and exit condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Connected, before loss | Center releases priority version P1; A, B and C confirm their accepted tasks, stock reservations and remaining capacity. Exercise controllers retain the agreed starting snapshot and participant acknowledgements. | Center approves P1; each team commits its own resources. If no offline authority/validity rules have been agreed, the exercise records that gap rather than assuming them. |
| 2. Communications unavailable | At exercise time T0, links become unavailable for a scheduled six-hour interval. Actors detect loss using an agreed timeout and record the last confirmed exchange; they attempt only approved alternative channels. | A missing acknowledgement is not consent. Detection timeout and alternative-channel ownership are open agreement items. |
| 3. Local degraded operation | Teams continue only tasks within their actual approved commitments and any agreed offline envelope, with local dispatch/delivery journals. Center marks remote status uncertain and records new priorities without pretending they were received. | No team acts as the center or commits another team's vehicles. A local record identifies author, event time and task ID, and distinguishes planned, dispatched and delivered. |
| 4. Stock/route inject | Team A finds fewer usable units than its record; B encounters a blocked route; C receives a locally reported urgent request. They record feasibility changes, delivery balances and any limits on continued work. | A and B use their own valid safety/resource authority; changes requiring priority approval wait. C cannot treat an unconfirmed report as a new approved priority. Proposed emergency escalation is exercised only if explicitly authorized beforehand. |
| 5. Staleness boundary | A controller injects an expired/conditional P1 task or a known destination change while the center's P2 update is unreachable. Teams expose the version and age of the information they are using. | Apply the agreed expiry/condition rule. Without one, the permitted action is unresolved; this draft proposes a hold on the affected new commitment and escalation, not invented authority to select priorities. Unaffected valid commitments need their own disposition. |
| 6. Link restored, reconciliation | At T0 + 6 hours in this exercise, exchange priority versions, commitments, dispatch/delivery receipts, remaining physical stock and unserved demand. Detect delayed/duplicate messages; preserve both conflicting entries with provenance. | A restored link does not authorize automatic replay or immediate normal dispatch. The center reconciles priority intent; each organization confirms its actual resources and actions. |
| 7. Disagreement and resumption | Suppose P2 assigns a task another team already completed and stock balances disagree. Confirm receipt/delivery evidence, release or correct affected reservations through the owning organization, and obtain a revised priority/commitment acknowledgement. | Center resolves its priority decision; stock owners resolve stock facts; disputed supply quality needs its designated authority. If factual/authority disagreement persists, escalate to the nominated parties and hold the affected allocation. No unilateral joint arbiter is assumed. Resume each reconciled task only after the relevant approvals and team acceptance. |

The exercise evidence package needs the actual authority/delegation records; roster and alternates; priority/validity and dispute rules; common item/unit mappings; vehicle and handling constraints; starting stock/commitments; scenario/inject schedule; proposed stop conditions; and participant-approved expected actions. Controllers should retain timed messages, acknowledgements, local journals, delivery receipts, discrepancy decisions and final balances. Compare approved versus committed/dispatched/delivered quantities, duplicate tasks, authorization/version errors, unmet demand, detection/reconciliation time and staff workload. Set acceptable limits with the participants before claiming success; no target values were supplied.

A tabletop can establish whether the organizations interpret the agreement consistently. A later communications-loss drill and representative logistics exercise can assess recording, capacity and handover behavior. Neither a draft nor a successful tabletop demonstrates actual flood delivery performance. Final conditions are either reconciled commitments with explicitly approved resumption, or an identified unresolved allocation with custody, owner and escalation recorded.

Evidence status: all arrangements and exercise activities above are proposed; no agreement, dispatch, exercise or test has occurred. No external policy or military acquisition basis was imported. Six cases shared one participant context, so this was not a separately isolated exercise run.
