Conditionally proceed with a concept that connects heterogeneous existing services while retaining accountable institutional ownership. Reopen identity, personal-data access, security, and accountability as core design questions before allowing the concept to handle real benefit cases.

The historical basis is the request-supplied account of the 1989 CERN WWW proposal. I have not independently retrieved it. Its priorities arose in that proposal's setting; they do not establish suitable priorities for a public benefits service.

The transferable ideas are useful: provide consistent discovery across different systems; connect to existing information rather than requiring wholesale replacement; use explicit interface meanings; and let responsible institutions maintain their authoritative information. These ideas could reduce duplicate data entry and help applicants navigate fragmented services. Those benefits remain hypotheses to evaluate with applicants and staff.

The assumptions requiring new decisions are consequential:

| Question to reopen | Why it changes this concept |
| --- | --- |
| Who may see which person's information, for what purpose? | A reachable link or a connected institution must not automatically confer access to a benefit case. Specify permitted data flows, minimization, retention, and the applicable basis for sharing with accountable owners. |
| How are identity, delegation, and authority distinguished? | Knowing a person's identity does not prove entitlement to another person's records or authority to decide a benefit. Define applicant, representative, caseworker, and institutional permissions, including expiry and revocation. |
| Which institution owns each fact and decision? | Data exchange must preserve provenance, correction routes, and responsibility. Conflicting or stale records need a disposition process; integration must not silently create adjudication authority. |
| How will people obtain service during exceptions? | Account recovery, inability to use digital identity, outages, disputed records, and handovers need staffed routes that preserve the applicant's ability to pursue the service. |
| Who can reconstruct and challenge an outcome? | Record accountable access and decision events, with appropriate protection of those records, and provide review and correction mechanisms. |

A candidate boundary is public access to general guidance, with controlled access to personal case work. Keep authorization effective at each agency interface, maintain source provenance, and assign explicit responsibility for decisions. Decentralized ownership and a consistent user experience can coexist, but only if agencies agree those contracts and can support the work.

The service owner can authorize a concept prototype using synthetic cases while owners resolve the data/role matrix, delegation rules, authoritative sources, correction process, and assisted-service model. Before real personal-data use, require evidence from scenarios covering legitimate access, cross-household access denial, representative revocation, conflicting records, agency outage, and shift handover. Evaluate both privacy protection and successful access to the service; a technically secure path that eligible users cannot complete is inadequate.

Proceed to operational use only for the scope whose responsibilities and evidence have been accepted by the appropriate owners. No jurisdiction, legal basis, institutional agreement, usability result, or security test is supplied here, so this is a conditional concept recommendation, not a compliance or deployment finding.
