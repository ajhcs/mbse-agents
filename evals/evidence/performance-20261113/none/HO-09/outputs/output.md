The outcome is reliable drinking-water service through seasonal demand changes and treatment maintenance. A lower unit production cost is useful only within a concept that delivers that service. Plant size is a means, not the outcome.

The stated staffing limit is two locations; confirm whether this means two continuously staffed sites, two sites in total, or a limit on the available operator roster before treating unattended units as feasible. Storm road closures are an operating condition that constrains replenishment, repair and access. Seasonal demand is a design condition requiring an actual demand profile. The sponsor's preference for the largest plant and its reported unit-cost advantage are preferences, not a requirement to select it. No interruption tolerance, storage volume, outage duration, capital budget or alternative cost is supplied.

I would first reconstruct a few representative maintenance and storm scenarios using plant outage records, repair/access delays, seasonal demand and the distribution layout. Agree how much interruption, if any, the sponsor will accept and whether that applies to all demand or an explicitly agreed essential-service level. Check the full path from source to customer: independent treatment or a full tank will not help if the same failed pump or main prevents delivery.

The question most likely to change the recommendation is: **Can usable, potable and deliverable storage cover the demand deficit throughout the longest outage the district agrees it must withstand, including storm-delayed repair, and refill before another such outage?** Compare usable volume with the time-integrated deficit between required delivery and available production, including an explicitly justified reserve. The necessary values are not supplied.

If that bridge is feasible and the delivery path remains available, investigate storage with maintainability improvements to the existing plant first. If it is infeasible, investigate independent treatment capacity that can be maintained within the two-location staffing limit, with storage for transition periods. Compare the largest central proposal on the same service scenarios, whole-service cost and maintenance coverage before selecting it. No option is established as cheapest or compliant from the given facts.
