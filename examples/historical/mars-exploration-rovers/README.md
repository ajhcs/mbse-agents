# Mars Exploration Rovers: connect scientific intent to operational choices

**HG-07 · 2004 · space · success with lifecycle adaptation**

Turn a broad scientific question into a sequence of feasible observations, then manage the life of that capability.

All `HG-` identifiers are new teaching identifiers. Historical facts below
are attributed to sources; the boundary, decisions, artifact, questions, and
transfer exercise are newly authored. No local historical simulation was performed.

## Source-grounded facts

[HG-07-S1](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/nasa-will-send-two-robotic-geologists-to-roam-on-mars/); Landing-site and instrument-workflow paragraphs.

## Retrospective systems analysis

**Boundary.** Science team, target selection, terrain, mobility, instruments, solar energy, communications, and software maintenance form an observation-producing system.

**Interfaces to examine**

- Science question → target selection: a measurement must discriminate among scientific explanations.
- Target priority → daily plan: traverse cost, power, hazards, and data return constrain achievable science.

**Decision.** How should a team compare a scientifically attractive target with a safer accessible one?

- Rank targets only by expected scientific interest.
- Compare information value with access cost, risk, energy, and remaining opportunities.

**Teaching recommendation.** Make the rejected option and its opportunity cost visible in the plan.

**Uncertainty.** Long life alone does not establish scientific effectiveness; compare returned observations with the scientific question.

## HG-07-A1: Observation-to-operation trade

This proposed artifact is a compact starting point for review. Its evidence
requests are unexecuted; its rows are not recovered project requirements.

| Item | Proposed disposition | Evidence to seek |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Remote survey | Select targets using discriminating features | Images, spectral clues, and alternative explanations |
| Close examination | Budget traverse, contact, power, and downlink together | Terrain and instrument feasibility checks |
| Extended operations | Reassess goals when capability changes | Observed resets, updated constraints, and recoverability evidence |

## Practice engineering judgment

1. Which scientific need is met by having both remote and contact instruments?
2. How could a technically successful traverse be a poor mission decision?
3. Write an extension recommendation with a condition that would reverse it.

**Reasoning signals for a formative review**

- Traces instruments to information needs rather than listing hardware.
- Treats resources and opportunities as coupled.
- Distinguishes design life, actual survival, and delivered mission value.

**Transfer exercise.** Synthetic: a survey robot has one day of battery remaining and three uncertain targets. Present a decision table without inventing probabilities.

**Limits.** The proposed trade is new. No rover science ranking, traverse model, or flight software experiment was executed here.
