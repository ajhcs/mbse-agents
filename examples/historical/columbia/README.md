# Columbia: keep anomalies visible across organizational boundaries

**HG-06 · 2003 · space · failure**

Successful previous operations do not resolve an unexplained hazard; information flow is part of system performance.

All `HG-` identifiers are new teaching identifiers. Historical facts below
are attributed to sources; the boundary, decisions, artifact, questions, and
transfer exercise are newly authored. No local historical simulation was performed.

## Source-grounded facts

- **HG-06-F1:** The board examined the physical damage from debris together with organizational conditions that shaped risk interpretation and decisions. [HG-06-S1](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/columbia/reports/CAIBreportv1.pdf); Chapters 6–7.
- **HG-06-F2:** It identified reliance on past success, communication barriers, inhibited dissent, and weak integration of safety information among organizational contributors. [HG-06-S1](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/columbia/reports/CAIBreportv1.pdf); Chapter 7, introduction and sections 7.1–7.6.

## Retrospective systems analysis

**Boundary.** Vehicle damage, imagery requests, analysis assumptions, mission management, technical authority, and possible response options define the in-flight assessment system.

**Interfaces to examine**

- Observation → analysis: uncertainty about damage must remain visible through analytical approximations.
- Analysis → management: the decision need includes what is unknown and what additional information could change.

**Decision.** How should repeated anomalies affect readiness and in-flight assessment?

- Use the history of successful missions to close the concern.
- Reassess the hazard, identify missing observations, and track assumptions to their decision consequence.

**Teaching recommendation.** Require a mechanism-based explanation before treating recurrence as acceptable.

**Uncertainty.** Better information does not guarantee a successful rescue or repair; feasibility needs separate evidence.

## Practice engineering judgment

1. What makes a previous anomaly informative about the next flight?
2. How can a request for more data be translated into a concrete decision need?
3. Write a status update that preserves technical uncertainty across management levels.

**Reasoning signals for a formative review**

- Avoids normalizing an unexplained event from favorable outcomes.
- Separates obtaining information from proving a viable response.
- Links communication structure to missing or distorted evidence.

**Transfer exercise.** Synthetic: an autonomous vehicle reports recurring unexplained impact indications. Produce a one-page issue disposition with reopening criteria.

**Limits.** The gallery does not assert that a particular counterfactual intervention would have saved the crew.
