# London 2012: separate event readiness from legacy value

**HG-20 · 2012 · public services · successful delivery with open legacy**

A program can deliver its immediate service while long-term benefits remain separate claims with different owners and evidence.

All `HG-` identifiers are new teaching identifiers. Historical facts below
are attributed to sources; the boundary, decisions, artifact, questions, and
transfer exercise are newly authored. No local historical simulation was performed.

## Source-grounded facts

- **HG-20-F1:** The NAO described successful staging, intensive scenario testing, and changed governance as planning transitioned to operations. [HG-20-S1](https://www.nao.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2012/12/1213794fr.pdf); Summary paragraphs 5–6.
- **HG-20-F2:** It reported effective contingency action for security staffing shortfalls while stating that the effectiveness of new legacy arrangements remained to be seen. [HG-20-S1](https://www.nao.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2012/12/1213794fr.pdf); Summary paragraphs 8–10.

## Retrospective systems analysis

**Boundary.** Venues, transport, volunteers, security, delivery organizations, government decision bodies, and future venue operators span different lifecycle phases.

**Interfaces to examine**

- Planning governance → operational command: decision speed and authority change at the event boundary.
- Event delivery → legacy operation: assets, funding, obligations, and benefit measurement need a new owner.

**Decision.** What should a post-event success statement include?

- Collapse delivery, cost forecasts, and future benefits into one success rating.
- Report achieved service, residual liabilities, forecasts, and long-term benefits as separate claims.

**Teaching recommendation.** Keep a benefits register alive after the delivery organization winds down.

**Uncertainty.** Effective contingency may impose costs or dependencies elsewhere; successful staging does not settle every value-for-money question.

## Practice engineering judgment

1. Which measures expire when the event ends?
2. What makes a contingency operationally credible?
3. Write a success statement that is positive without claiming unrealized benefits.

**Reasoning signals for a formative review**

- Separates immediate outputs, operating outcomes, and later benefits.
- Connects governance and resource readiness to contingencies.
- Retains accountability after organizational transition.

**Transfer exercise.** Synthetic: a city completes an emergency-response facility before a major event. Draft separate opening and five-year benefit acceptance statements.

**Limits.** The case uses the 2012 audit’s knowledge state; it does not assert the eventual outcome of every legacy commitment.
