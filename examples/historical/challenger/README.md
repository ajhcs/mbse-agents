# Challenger: make dissent reach the decision

**HG-02 · 1986 · space · failure**

Treat readiness as an evidence and communication process with identifiable decision authority.

All `HG-` identifiers are new teaching identifiers. Historical facts below
are attributed to sources; the boundary, decisions, artifact, questions, and
transfer exercise are newly authored. No local historical simulation was performed.

## Source-grounded facts

- **HG-02-F1:** The commission found that launch decision-makers lacked important information about O-ring problems, the initial contractor recommendation, and continuing engineering objections. [HG-02-S1](https://www.nasa.gov/history/rogersrep/v1ch5.htm); Chapter V, pp. 82–83.
- **HG-02-F2:** The formal readiness structure allowed concerns to bypass key managers despite review procedures having been followed. [HG-02-S1](https://www.nasa.gov/history/rogersrep/v1ch5.htm); Chapter V, pp. 82–84.

## Retrospective systems analysis

**Boundary.** The readiness system includes contractors, engineering judgment, launch management, environmental conditions, and escalation paths.

**Interfaces to examine**

- Engineering → launch authority: uncertainty and disagreement must survive management summarization.
- Previous flight evidence → current decision: past operation is bounded by the conditions actually observed.

**Decision.** What should a readiness board receive when specialists and management disagree?

- A single consolidated recommendation.
- A recommendation plus unresolved dissent, its evidence, and the authority accepting the risk.

**Teaching recommendation.** Preserve the technical issue and its disposition as an explicit decision input.

**Uncertainty.** A new escalation form alone cannot establish that concerns will be heard or resolved.

## HG-02-A1: Dissent disposition record

This proposed artifact is a compact starting point for review. Its evidence
requests are unexecuted; its rows are not recovered project requirements.

| Item | Proposed disposition | Evidence to seek |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Concern | State mechanism, affected function, and disputed condition | Underlying observations and missing-condition evidence |
| Disagreement | Retain competing interpretations and named technical roles | Direct review with decision authority |
| Disposition | Record decision, rationale, owner, and reopening trigger | Acknowledgment that dissent reached the deciding level |

## Practice engineering judgment

1. What information would be lost in a red/amber/green readiness roll-up?
2. When does previous success provide relevant evidence, and when does it fail to?
3. Rewrite a vague concern into a decision request without overstating certainty.

**Reasoning signals for a formative review**

- Separates the absence of observed failure from demonstrated operating margin.
- Retains dissent without treating every disagreement as equally supported.
- Names who decides and what evidence would change the decision.

**Transfer exercise.** Synthetic: a launch-independent field deployment faces an environmental condition outside tested experience. Write the unresolved-issue paragraph for its readiness review.

**Limits.** This case does not infer outside political intervention or reduce the accident to one individual decision.
