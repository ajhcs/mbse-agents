# Boston tunnel ceiling: match evidence to duration and use

**HG-11 · 2006 · public infrastructure · failure**

A short acceptance test cannot close a long-duration load claim without a justified model of time-dependent behavior.

All `HG-` identifiers are new teaching identifiers. Historical facts below
are attributed to sources; the boundary, decisions, artifact, questions, and
transfer exercise are newly authored. No local historical simulation was performed.

## Source-grounded facts

- **HG-11-F1:** The NTSB attributed the ceiling collapse to epoxy anchor adhesive with inadequate creep resistance under sustained loading. [HG-11-S1](https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/AccidentReports/Reports/HAR0702.pdf); Probable Cause, p. 107.
- **HG-11-F2:** Its findings connected material selection and approval to insufficient consideration of long-term adhesive behavior. [HG-11-S1](https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/AccidentReports/Reports/HAR0702.pdf); Executive summary; Conclusions.

## Retrospective systems analysis

**Boundary.** Adhesive, anchors, concrete, sustained ceiling load, installation, supplier information, design approval, and inspection form the support system.

**Interfaces to examine**

- Material qualification → structural use: load duration and environment must match the claimed application.
- Installation acceptance → service inspection: passing a local test does not remove degradation mechanisms.

**Decision.** Can a satisfactory short pull test establish the service-life claim?

- Treat load exceedance during the test as sufficient margin.
- Identify creep, aging, installation variability, and the evidence needed for the sustained load.

**Teaching recommendation.** Require a duration-appropriate basis and a review of changes in product or use.

**Uncertainty.** A proposed accelerated test needs a justified relation to service behavior; it is not automatically conservative.

## HG-11-A1: Evidence applicability check

This proposed artifact is a compact starting point for review. Its evidence
requests are unexecuted; its rows are not recovered project requirements.

| Item | Proposed disposition | Evidence to seek |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Load duration | Separate short proof load from sustained demand | Creep data and justified extrapolation |
| Installed condition | Define preparation, cure, temperature, and variability | Installation records and representative testing |
| In-service condition | Set detectable degradation and response criteria | Inspection capability and intervention plan |

## Practice engineering judgment

1. What hidden assumption connects a brief test to a long service life?
2. Which supplier or installation change should reopen qualification?
3. Explain why a passed test may be valid evidence for a narrower claim.

**Reasoning signals for a formative review**

- Names the time-dependent failure mechanism.
- Defines applicability rather than rejecting testing wholesale.
- Connects procurement, installation, and maintenance evidence.

**Transfer exercise.** Synthetic: an adhesive attachment passes a one-minute proof test but must support a constant load for years. Draft the unresolved claim and evidence plan.

**Limits.** No structural analysis or adhesive lifetime prediction was executed; this is not a construction acceptance basis.
