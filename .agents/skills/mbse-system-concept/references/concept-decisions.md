# Concept decisions

Keep enough of the comparison for another engineer to check the choice. Useful fields include the stakeholder outcome, essential functions, candidate mechanism, operating assumptions, performance and lifecycle constraints, dependencies, evidence gaps, and cost of reversing the choice.

Keep constraints distinct from preferences. Use units and ranges for quantitative estimates and explain their basis. A weighted score is a decision aid only after criteria and weights are agreed; do not hide a failed hard constraint inside an attractive average.

For a feasibility experiment, identify the uncertain claim, measurement, representative environment, acceptance condition, and the decision it unlocks. At handoff, report which uncertainty the prototype addresses and which lifecycle or operational claims still need evidence.

A concept record can be a short narrative plus an alternatives table. Stable IDs are useful when outcomes and constraints will become downstream requirements. Do not manufacture requirements from every exploratory observation.
